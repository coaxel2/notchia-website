Hi Wes,

I read your July 2024 piece on NotchNook — still the writeup I send to friends when they ask why their MacBook's black bar should do something. I'm writing because there's a new entry in that same category and I think it has a story angle worth a short look.

I built NotchIA, an independent macOS app, alone from Paris and shipped it publicly this spring. It does what you'd expect from a notch utility — music controls, calendar, file drag-and-drop — but two things make it feel like a different kind of app:

1. When you're using Claude Code, ChatGPT Codex or GitHub Copilot, the notch becomes a live status bar for whichever assistant is currently working. You can glance up and see whether it's reading code, waiting for your permission, or burning through your hourly quota — without leaving your editor. It's the first time I've seen a notch app treat AI assistants as a system-level thing, not a separate app.

2. There's an RSS reader and a PDF summarizer that use Apple Intelligence on-device. No cloud, nothing leaves the laptop. Drop a PDF into the notch and get a summary back, locally. It's one of the rare consumer apps actually leaning on the Foundation Models that shipped with macOS 26.

- Free Essential tier; lifetime Pro for 39.99 EUR (no subscription)

- UI in English, French, Spanish, German

Site: https://notchia.app/
